Introduction to Anatomy and Physiology

Complete nursing study notes in simple English.

1. Introduction

The human body is a complex living structure made up of cells, tissues, organs and organ systems. All these structures work together to maintain life.

Anatomy and Physiology are two basic subjects of medical and nursing science. They help nurses understand the structure and functions of the human body.

2. Definition of Anatomy

Anatomy is the branch of science that deals with the study of the structure, shape, size, location and relationship of different parts of the human body.

Example: Studying the structure of the heart, lungs, brain and kidneys.

3. Definition of Physiology

Physiology is the branch of science that deals with the study of the normal functions and activities of different parts of the human body.

Example: Studying how the heart pumps blood and how the lungs exchange gases.

4. Difference Between Anatomy and Physiology

AnatomyPhysiology
Study of body structureStudy of body functions
Explains what the body looks likeExplains how the body works
Studies organs and tissuesStudies activities of organs
Example: Structure of heartExample: Pumping action of heart

5. Branches of Anatomy

  1. Gross Anatomy: Study of body structures visible to the naked eye.
  2. Microscopic Anatomy: Study of structures that require a microscope.
  3. Regional Anatomy: Study of all structures in a particular body region.
  4. Systemic Anatomy: Study of the body according to organ systems.
  5. Surface Anatomy: Study of external body features and their relationship with internal structures.
  6. Developmental Anatomy: Study of structural changes throughout life.
  7. Embryology: Study of development of the embryo.
  8. Applied Anatomy: Application of anatomical knowledge in medical practice.

6. Branches of Physiology

  1. Cell Physiology: Study of normal functions of cells.
  2. Human Physiology: Study of normal functions of the human body.
  3. Neurophysiology: Study of the functions of the nervous system.
  4. Cardiovascular Physiology: Study of the heart and blood circulation.
  5. Respiratory Physiology: Study of breathing and gas exchange.
  6. Renal Physiology: Study of kidney functions.
  7. Endocrine Physiology: Study of hormones and endocrine glands.
  8. Reproductive Physiology: Study of reproductive functions.

6. Organism Level
  1. Chemical Level: Atoms and molecules form the basic building blocks of the body.
  2. Cellular Level: Molecules form cells, the basic structural and functional units of life.
  3. Tissue Level: Groups of similar cells work together to perform a specific function.
  4. Organ Level: Different tissues combine to form an organ.
  5. Organ System Level: Different organs work together to perform major body functions.
  6. Organism Level: All organ systems work together to form a complete human being.

9. Anatomical Position

The anatomical position is the standard reference position used to describe the location of body parts.

  • Body standing upright.
  • Head and eyes facing forward.
  • Arms placed at the sides.
  • Palms facing forward.
  • Feet flat and directed forward.
Remember: Anatomical position is the standard reference position in anatomy.

11. Body Planes

  1. Sagittal Plane: Divides the body into right and left parts.
  2. Frontal (Coronal) Plane: Divides the body into anterior and posterior parts.
  3. Transverse (Horizontal) Plane: Divides the body into upper and lower parts.
Sagittal Plane: Right | Left
Frontal Plane: Front | Back
Transverse Plane: Upper | Lower

12. Major Body Cavities

A. Dorsal Body Cavity

  • Cranial Cavity: Contains the brain.
  • Vertebral Cavity: Contains the spinal cord.

B. Ventral Body Cavity

  • Thoracic Cavity: Contains the heart and lungs.
  • Abdominal Cavity: Contains the stomach, liver, intestines and other organs.
  • Pelvic Cavity: Contains the urinary bladder, reproductive organs and rectum.

13. Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the ability of the body to maintain a relatively stable internal environment despite changes inside or outside the body.

Examples

  • Regulation of body temperature.
  • Maintenance of blood glucose levels.
  • Regulation of blood pressure.
  • Maintenance of fluid and electrolyte balance.

Negative Feedback

Negative feedback is a response that reverses a change and brings the body towards its normal condition.

Example: When body temperature increases, sweating helps cool the body.

14. Importance of Anatomy and Physiology in Nursing

  1. Helps nurses understand normal body structure and function.
  2. Helps identify abnormal signs and symptoms.
  3. Supports accurate patient assessment.
  4. Helps in understanding diseases and complications.
  5. Improves safe administration of medications.
  6. Helps in planning appropriate nursing care.
  7. Supports patient education and health promotion.
  8. Helps nurses understand diagnostic and laboratory reports.
